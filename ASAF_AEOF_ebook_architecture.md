# ASAF and AEOF Ebook Architecture Blueprint

**Updated:** June 8, 2026  
**Purpose:** Convert the existing ASAF and AEOF framework assets into two distinct, high-value ebooks that feel like related flagship works rather than exported framework documentation.

---

## At a glance

### The recommendation in one sentence

Use a **shared editorial architecture** across both books, but use a **different source strategy** for each:

- **ASAF ebook** → convert the existing market-insight note series into a continuous manuscript
- **AEOF ebook** → author a manuscript directly from the framework schema and transformation journey

### The fundamental distinction

| Framework | Current source form | Editorial challenge | Best build method |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ASAF** | Market insight series | Too modular, too standalone, too note-like | **Editorial conversion** |
| **AEOF** | Schema + transformation model | Too structural, not yet narrative enough | **Manuscript authoring** |

### Bottom-line strategy

- **ASAF = editorial conversion problem**
- **AEOF = authoring and packaging problem**

## Why this document exists

This document is not just a table of contents sketch. It is meant to function as a **working editorial brief** for building two separate ebooks from the frameworks already in the workspace.

It answers five practical questions:

1. What should the two books **be about**, at a publishing level?
2. How should they be **structured**?
3. What content should be **kept, removed, moved, or expanded**?
4. How should the books differ so they feel like **siblings, not clones**?
5. What are the next concrete assets needed to move from framework to manuscript?

---

## The real change required

The current ASAF materials are optimized for **modular reading**. A strong ebook is optimized for **sequential reading, retention, and cumulative argument**.

That means both books should shift from this:

- standalone note logic
- repeated metadata and reference tables
- modular audience-entry framing
- framework mechanics embedded directly in chapter flow

To this:

- narrative continuity
- escalating chapter progression
- stronger transitions and synthesis
- more practical examples
- cleaner chapter openings and endings
- appendices carrying the heavier framework-reference burden

### The big editorial move

> A note series asks, “Can each piece survive on its own?”  
> A book asks, “Why should the reader keep turning the page?”

That is the decisive shift.

---

## Success criteria for both ebooks

An effective ebook version of either framework should do all of the following:

- make the framework feel **strategic, not merely taxonomic**
- explain **why the old operating model is breaking**
- show **what replaces it**
- help a reader understand **where they fit in the new model**
- feel readable **front-to-back**, not only as a reference object
- still preserve the rigor of the underlying framework

### If the books are working, the reader should leave with:

- a clear mental model
- a memorable architecture
- a more precise language for the shift
- a practical set of implications
- a sense that the framework is usable, not academic

---

## Shared editorial rules for both books

Both books should follow the same editorial rules, even though their source material differs.

### 1. Start with the operating-model problem, not the framework diagram

The opening chapters should first explain:

- what is ending
- why it is ending
- what is replacing it
- why the change is structural rather than incremental

Only then should the framework be introduced as the organizing model.

### 2. Optimize for continuity, not modularity

Every chapter should inherit from the previous one.

That means removing or minimizing chapter furniture that exists only because the original materials were modular, such as:

- “Depends On”
- “Enables”
- “Standalone?”
- repetitive framework-reference tables in every chapter

### 3. Use a repeatable chapter logic

Each chapter should follow this pattern:

1. **The claim** — one sharp, falsifiable thesis
2. **The failure of the old model** — what the chapter replaces
3. **Framework evidence** — the logic from stages, dimensions, and principles
4. **Market / enterprise implications** — who must act differently
5. **Reader payoff** — what the chapter clarifies
6. **Transition** — why the next chapter follows naturally

### 4. Move detail-heavy framework material to appendices

The main manuscript should prioritize:

- argument
- explanation
- synthesis
- implication
- memorable examples

The appendices should carry:

- full principle sets
- stage descriptors
- dimension / sub-dimension reference
- scoring methods
- worksheets and checklists

### 5. Add recurring payoff devices

Every chapter should include a compact reader-facing payoff device such as:

- **Why this matters**
- **If you are a CISO / CIO / COO / product leader / architect**
- **What changes now**
- **What to look for in vendors / platforms**

### 6. Add concrete operating-model examples

Each chapter should contain at least one of the following:

- a buyer scenario
- a vendor briefing diagnostic
- a before/after operating model snapshot
- a maturity transition example
- a practical architecture vignette

### 7. End with momentum, not just closure

Every chapter should end with:

- three takeaways
- two implications
- one bridge to the next chapter

That small rhythm does enormous work in ebook readability.

# ASAF ebook blueprint

## Strategic role of the ASAF book

The ASAF ebook should be the **security-operations thesis book**.

Its job is not simply to describe agentic security operations. Its job is to make the case that:

- the tiered SOC model is ending
- the replacement model is architectural, not incremental
- the replacement can be described, staged, and assessed
- vendor strategy and buyer decisions should be judged against this shift

### Ideal reader takeaway

The ASAF reader should finish the book believing:

> “The old security operations model is structurally obsolete, and I now have a vocabulary and framework for understanding what replaces it.”

---

### Working title options

- **The Agentic SOC**
- **The End of the Tiered SOC**
- **Agentic Security Operations**
- **From Tiered Defense to Governed Autonomous Security Operations**

### Working subtitle options

- A framework for the post-analyst operating model
- How security operations becomes a governed control system
- Designing the shift from alert queues to agentic security operations

### Positioning note

The strongest ASAF titles are those that emphasize **structural replacement**, not generic AI modernization. This book should feel like a category-defining point of view, not a handbook on tooling trends.

- a capstone integration model

# AEOF ebook blueprint

## Strategic role of the AEOF book

The AEOF ebook should be the **enterprise-operations operating-model book**.

Its job is to make the case that:

- queue-centric operations is not the future of IT / service / platform operations
- tickets, hand-offs, and escalations are a decaying coordination model
- governed autonomy becomes the next operating baseline
- enterprises need a framework for assessing the move from assisted operations to agentic service control

### Ideal reader takeaway

The AEOF reader should finish the book believing:

> “The future of enterprise operations is not faster ticket processing; it is a governed, graph-aware, agentic operating model.”

---

## Recommended AEOF positioning

### Working title options

- **The Agentic Operations Model**
- **Beyond Queues and Escalations**
- **Agentic Enterprise Operations**
- **From Tickets to Governed Autonomous Operations**

### Working subtitle options

- A framework for post-tier IT and service operations
- How enterprise operations becomes a governed service control system
- Designing the shift from queue-centric operations to agentic execution

### Positioning note

The strongest AEOF titles emphasize **operating-model transformation**, not generic IT automation. This book should feel like a statement about the future of enterprise operations, not a modernization playbook for service desks.

## Best manuscript strategy for AEOF

AEOF should be written as a full book from the start. It should **not** be forced into the ASAF note-series mold.

Instead, author it directly from:

- principles

- failure modes

### Key distinction from ASAF

ASAF already has a usable note-series spine.  
AEOF currently has a **schema spine**, which is valuable—but schemas do not narrate themselves. Charming, but alas, they rarely write the introduction.

Because AEOF does not yet have note-series prose comparable to ASAF, each AEOF chapter should be drafted using a standard pattern.

### Recommended drafting template

1. **Core position**  
   One declarative, falsifiable claim.

2. **The old operating-model failure**

3. **Framework evidence**  
   The relevant principle, dimension, stage, and sub-dimension logic.

4. **Enterprise implications**  
   What CIOs, COOs, service leaders, platform owners, or MSP operators should change.

5. **Platform / vendor implications**  
   What tools and providers must prove in the new model.

6. **Reader payoff**  
   What this chapter helps the reader see more clearly.

   What this chapter unlocks next.

# What must change to make the ebooks effective

## 1. Stop optimizing the body for modular reading

Book chapters can remain individually useful, but they should no longer carry the full burden of being independent analyst notes.

## 2. Move framework-heavy mechanics into appendices

The body should emphasize:

- argument
- explanation
- implication
- synthesis

The appendices should absorb:

- full stage descriptions
- full dimension / sub-dimension detail
- reference tables
- scoring methods
- assessment aids

## 3. Add transitions, synthesis, and chapter payoff

Every chapter should answer:

- what changed?
- why does it matter?
- what should the reader now believe?
- why does the next chapter follow?

## 4. Design visuals as memory anchors

Each chapter needs one primary visual that helps the reader remember the conceptual shift. A good visual in these books should not just explain; it should linger.

## 5. Add operating-model scenes

Both books need more scenes from real operating life, not just framework statements.

Examples:

- a Stage 0 team meeting vs a Stage 3 governance review
- a vendor claim translated into framework terms
- a maturity transition decision point
- a workflow before/after view

## 6. Strengthen the endings

Both books should land in practical leadership territory, not stop at conceptual closure.

The AEOF material is already strong enough conceptually to support a second ebook, but it needs a purpose-built manuscript structure rather than a direct export from the schema.
