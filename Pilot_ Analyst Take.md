

| What is an Analyst Take? An Analyst Take is a concise article (under 5-minute read \= 900-word max) that provides clients with candid perspectives, insights and advice from an individual Gartner analyst. UPDATED While we’re in pilot mode, we are allowing co-authors, but please be mindful about it. Because these are to be written in the first-person, you’ll need to think about how to distinguish one voice over the other in the post or consistently use “we” language. What are the goals in offering Analyst Takes? Provide clients with shorter, more digestible insights and advice. Give clients the opportunity to get to know the individual analysts behind Gartner’s human intelligence. Make it easier for analysts to express their unique perspectives on G.com in an unfiltered way. Introduce a more relatable, conversational voice​ on G.com.  What makes for a good Analyst Take? Written in the first person, using a conversational style. Covers a topic that has a business application and is something CxOs will care about (not purely personal). Presents a unique POV stemming from your experience and expertise, and this POV is only available on G.com (i.e., it is not cross-posted on LinkedIn). Provides clients with a clear, concrete outcome or next action and includes links to relevant Gartner resources. # **How to Write, Structure and Style an Analyst Take**

The table below details how to compile each section. The Section Heading column specifies whether each section is required or optional and clarifies if the heading in the CPP canvas will publish on the website or not.

| Section Heading | How to Write, Structure and Style It |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Title Required** *Header won’t publish*   | Begin your title with “Analyst Take:”… Aim for 65 characters maximum length Ensure the title accurately reflects the content Don’t duplicate titles of existing resources (e.g., if you are writing an Analyst Take related to a published piece of content, use a different title). *See ["How to Write Good Titles..."](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1T2WHE-2em--IqU1Q7y_fDfhX8mMmxD0RaIfYVsH_-lA/edit?usp=sharing) for additional tips* |
| **Summary Required** *Header won’t publish* | Use the following boilerplate formula to fulfill this section of the template, which will appear beneath the title in the Reader experience: Critical perspective and insight from \<ANALYST NAME\>, \<JOB TITLE\> **UPDATED** If you have a co-author, their name and title should be included here too. |
| **Body Text Required** *Header won’t publish* | This is the main text of your content. The structure is flexible to accommodate the many ways you can succinctly convey and organize your content. Target Length: 400-900 words (2-5 minute read time) Voice: Simple, conversational, first-person (“I” or “my”) Format your content so it’s easy for clients to scan and read – Avoid walls of text\! *See [Content Consumability Playbook](https://docs.google.com/presentation/u/0/d/1V6a4CUClc1KS_amWJn3AckqgsxUUjMtWIt-ge2SiUeA/edit) for more detailed tips and best practices* **Other Elements Optional** Graphics: Add graphics to visualize detailed evidence or illustrate an important, complex or novel concept *Visit the [Graphics Resource Center](https://inside.gartner.com/page/22981) for in-depth information on developing meaningful and consumable graphics.* |
| **Recommended by the Author Required** | Progressive reveal and client engagement is a core objective for an Analyst Take. Include a list of the most relevant, active Gartner content to provide more context or detail for your Analyst Take. |
| **Notes Optional** *Remove this section from the canvas if not needed* | If necessary, include Notes in this section to add detail/context specific to a point you are trying to make in your Analyst Take, but that isn’t essential to a reader’s understanding of the content.  Ensure all citations follow the [Gartner Style Guide](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1f8Vq7A5oPitRnPaP5-DdzvpmWHtLaSh4kqay3V7-Cwg/edit). |

# **Is Analyst Take right for you and your insights?**

| Analyst Take is a good choice when… | Don’t pick an Analyst Take if… |
| :---- | :---- |
| You want to provide your own advice or top-of-mind insights in your own words (i.e., first-person). You have some personal or anecdotal advice to offer that is complementary or additive to other published research. You have a unique point-of-view based on your experience as an analyst You want to encourage discussion or engagement with clients who’ll read your Take.  | You need to respond to an urgent event/issue within 24 hours.  Use [First Take](https://gartner365.sharepoint.com/:p:/s/IntranetContent/ERMFJLaxbFdLgpmGu10VqLcB7e9p_vqAnb9n28dX2ziQTA) instead. Your content is a “work in progress” that you’ll iterate on as topics or events evolve.  Use [Evolving Insights](https://gartner365.sharepoint.com/:p:/r/sites/ProjGravityRAThoughtLeadership/Shared%20Documents/General/Beta%20Research/Evolving%20Insights%20Resources/Evolving%20Insights%20-%20When%20and%20How%20to%20Use.pptx?d=w0370776876274a0fab7c2656dbb9ec0d&csf=1&web=1&e=8bAkJw) instead. You’re conveying **Gartner’s official position** on a topic and what actions clients need to take. Continue to use traditional content types. You want to provide extensive proprietary data, tools to help execute, cases-in-point, etc. that are not already published elsewhere.  Continue to use traditional content types. You’ve already published the same advice or point of view externally, such as via a LinkedIn post. *At this time, we are not cross-posting Analyst Take content outside of the paywall.* You want to make a forecast or pronouncement but data is not yet available to support it. You want to rank or compare vendors/offerings.  Continue to use our market/vendor content types. You want to share a topic you’re personally interested in without also including topics that C-level clients care about.  |

# **Appendix: Sample Ideation Agenda for Analyst Take**

| Objectives |
| :---- |
| ❏     **Target Role**: Who am I writing to? ❏     **Goals:** What’s the specific priority or problem this content helps the client solve? Where does it fit in their journey? What is my position and call to action? What am I hearing and why am I sharing this? |
| **Topic Sourcing** |
| ❏  **New ways of thinking about or approaching a problem:** How can I unpack and simplify issues for clients? ❏  **Seasonality:** What are most organizations focusing on in the coming month(s)? (i.e., is budgeting season coming up?) ❏  **Current Events:** Can you offer perspective on functional trends shaping our clients’ work? What is coming up more in inquiry/conference conversations and why? ❏  **Coverage/Variety:** Am I covering a healthy mix of topics that would appeal to our broadest cross section of our audience (i.e., supporting the perennial objectives of the CxO’s team)? ❏  **Benchmarking Trends:** Looking at benchmark data, what has changed and why? |
| **Materials** |
| ❏     **Cutting Room Floor:** What data and insights didn’t make it into my latest publication, but would be useful in a different or more standalone context? ❏     **Live Event Recaps:** What were your takeaways? ❏     **Upcoming Deliverables:** Is there any new content in the pipeline that’ll come out soon? Are there some insights you want to preview? |
| **Evergreen Insights** |
| ❏     **Greatest Hits Content:** What past insights continue to hold relevance and provide value as conventional wisdom challenged by the latest trends? |
