# Market Insight: AI Is Reshaping MDR Pricing, but Most Vendors Aren't Ready

## Summary

Agentic AI is changing the cost structure and value delivery of MDR services, yet most providers have not adjusted their pricing models to reflect this shift. Outcome-based pricing remains underadopted despite clear market signals that buyers want measurable results.

## Executive Summary

### Key Findings

- **Outcome-based pricing adoption is very low across the MDR market.** Of 95 vendors assessed, the average score for success and outcome-linked fee structures (PRC-SUC) is just 1.63 out of 5.0, with 82 vendors (86%) scoring 2.0 or below. Only two vendors score above 3.0 on this dimension. The median score is 1 (the lowest possible), indicating that most MDR providers have made no meaningful progress toward tying commercial terms to measurable client outcomes.

- **AI-mature vendors show measurably higher pricing sophistication.** Vendors with Significant or higher AI pricing influence (19 providers) average 2.86 across all pricing dimensions versus 1.84 for Minimal-influence vendors (27 providers), a 1.02-point gap on a 5-point scale. This correlation holds across every pricing dimension but is most pronounced in outcome alignment (PRC-OUT), where AI-mature vendors score 2.58 versus 1.04 for Minimal-tier providers.

- **The market misunderstands what outcome-based pricing means.** Most vendors equate "outcomes" with SLA uptime guarantees or basic breach notification speeds. True outcome-based pricing ties commercial terms to measurable security improvements: reduction in mean time to detect (MTTD), mean time to respond (MTTR), quantifiable risk posture improvements, or incident frequency reduction commitments backed by financial mechanisms such as breach warranties or performance-based fee adjustments.

- **Composable pricing models are a prerequisite for outcome-based evolution, but two-thirds of vendors remain subscription-only.** 64 of 95 vendors (67%) operate on pure subscription models, scoring an average of just 1.34 on PRC-SUC. The 27 composable-model vendors average 2.41 on the same dimension, nearly double. Outcome-based pricing requires the modularity and flexibility that subscription-only models cannot support.

- **AI is creating an efficiency dividend that vendors are capturing rather than sharing with buyers.** As AI-driven automation reduces the per-incident cost of detection and response, vendors are improving their margins without passing those efficiency gains through to clients via adjusted pricing mechanisms. The absence of usage-based alignment (PRC-USG averages just 2.14/5.0) means buyers have no visibility into how AI-driven efficiency improvements affect the cost of their service.

- **The pricing maturity gap between leaders and laggards is widening, driven primarily by AI adoption differences.** The top-10 vendors by overall pricing score average 3.65/5.0 while the market average is 2.35/5.0, a 1.30-point spread. The top performers uniformly show higher AI influence scores (averaging 3.5+), composable pricing models, and at least nascent outcome-linked commercial terms. This gap will widen as AI-first vendors use efficiency gains to fund outcome guarantees that subscription-only providers cannot match.

### Recommendations

- **Redefine outcome-based pricing in concrete, measurable terms.** Product leaders must move beyond SLA guarantees and establish commercial structures tied to quantifiable security outcomes (MTTD/MTTR improvement commitments, coverage expansion metrics, incident frequency reduction targets, and risk score improvements) backed by financial mechanisms such as breach warranties, performance credits, or shared-savings arrangements. Start with a single outcome metric tied to a fee adjustment and expand from there.

- **Build an AI efficiency-sharing pricing layer that demonstrates value to buyers.** Establish transparent mechanisms (efficiency dashboards, cost-per-incident trending, or AI utilization reporting) that show buyers how AI-driven automation is reducing service delivery costs. Tie at least a portion of pricing to these efficiency metrics through consumption-based components or graduated discount structures. Vendors that share the AI dividend early will secure longer contract terms and higher retention.

- **Transition from subscription-only to composable pricing architectures.** Begin decomposing monolithic subscription tiers into modular service components (base detection, response orchestration, threat hunting, incident retainer, and outcome guarantees) that buyers can assemble based on their specific risk profile. Our data shows composable vendors score 2.80 versus 2.16 for subscription-only across all pricing dimensions (a 30% premium), and the gap on outcome-linked pricing is even wider (2.41 vs. 1.34).

- **Invest in AI pricing-influence capabilities as a direct commercial differentiator.** The 1.02-point pricing sophistication gap between AI-mature and AI-minimal vendors is the single strongest predictor of overall pricing model maturity. Product leaders should treat AI investment not just as a cost efficiency play but as the enabler of commercial model innovation, from usage-based billing driven by AI workload telemetry to outcome guarantees underwritten by AI-validated detection capabilities.

- **Develop a phased roadmap from transparency to outcomes, anchored to AI maturity milestones.** Most vendors cannot leap directly from subscription-only pricing to full outcome-based models. The progression should follow a clear sequence: (1) publish transparent pricing and tier definitions, (2) introduce usage-aligned components tied to data volume or endpoint count, (3) add composable modules with mix-and-match flexibility, (4) introduce limited outcome commitments (e.g., MTTR guarantees with service credits), and (5) implement full risk-sharing or performance-linked fee structures. Each stage should be unlocked by corresponding AI capability maturity.

- **Establish competitive pricing intelligence as a standing practice, not a periodic exercise.** With a 1.30-point spread between top-10 and market-average pricing scores, and AI influence accelerating the divergence, vendors operating without structured pricing benchmarking are flying blind. Product leaders should institute quarterly pricing model assessments that benchmark their position on each pricing dimension against the market, track the velocity of competitor pricing evolution, and identify the specific AI capabilities driving commercial model changes in leading vendors.

## Strategic Planning Assumption

By 2028, 40% of MDR contracts will include at least one outcome-linked pricing component tied to measurable security improvements, up from less than 5% today. Vendors that fail to develop outcome-based commercial models by 2027 will face a structural competitive disadvantage in retention and new business.

## Analysis

### What Outcome-Based Pricing Actually Means, and Why the Market Gets It Wrong

The term "outcome-based pricing" has become a marketing fixture in managed detection and response. Vendors routinely cite SLA-backed response times or uptime guarantees as evidence of outcome alignment. This misrepresents what outcome-based pricing requires.

True outcome-based pricing establishes a direct financial link between what the buyer pays and the measurable security outcomes they receive. This means commercial terms that adjust, up or down, based on metrics the buyer actually cares about: How fast are threats detected and contained? Is the organization's attack surface actually shrinking? Are incidents becoming less frequent, less severe, or both? Is the cost of security per unit of business value improving over time?

In practice, this can take several forms:

- **Breach warranties**: Financial guarantees that pay out if a covered breach occurs, underwritten by the vendor's confidence in their detection and response capabilities.
- **Performance-linked fee adjustments**: Pricing that flexes based on achieved MTTD/MTTR improvements, with defined baselines and measurement periods.
- **Risk-score-tied subscriptions**: Pricing tied to an independently measured risk posture score, where improvement reduces the fee and degradation increases it.
- **Shared savings models**: Arrangements where a portion of the vendor's fee is tied to demonstrated cost avoidance or efficiency improvements for the buyer.
- **Incident severity caps**: Commercial terms that cap the buyer's financial exposure for incidents above a defined severity threshold.

Our assessment of 95 MDR vendors found that PRC-SUC (success and outcome fees) is the weakest pricing dimension in the market, averaging just 1.63 out of 5.0, nearly half the score of subscription transparency (3.52). The median is 1.0, meaning the typical MDR vendor has no outcome-linked pricing mechanism whatsoever. Even among the top 10 vendors on this dimension, the average is only 3.20, indicating that even leaders are in early stages. The two vendors that score above 3.0 on PRC-SUC, Accenture Security and Deloitte Cyber, are both large consultancies with composable models, not pureplay MDR providers.

### Why Outcome-Based Pricing Adoption Lags So Far Behind

Several structural and organizational factors explain the sluggish adoption of outcome-based pricing, despite clear buyer demand:

**1. Lack of measurement infrastructure.** Outcome-based pricing requires metrics that both parties trust. Most MDR providers lack the telemetry, benchmarking, and reporting infrastructure to measure and attribute outcomes with enough rigor to tie commercial terms to them. Without normalized baselines for MTTD, MTTR, incident frequency, or coverage breadth, outcome commitments cannot be quantified.

**2. Revenue predictability addiction.** Subscription-only models provide smooth, predictable revenue streams that are rewarded by investors and simplify financial planning. Outcome-based components introduce variability into the revenue model, which finance teams and boards resist. 64 of 95 vendors (67%) remain subscription-only, and their average overall pricing score of 2.16 (versus 2.80 for composable vendors) indicates that the subscription model itself constrains pricing sophistication.

**3. Insufficient AI capability to underwrite guarantees.** Outcome-based pricing requires confidence in delivery. A vendor cannot offer a breach warranty or MTTR guarantee without the AI-driven detection and response capabilities to consistently achieve them. Our data shows this directly: vendors with Significant AI influence score 2.16 on PRC-SUC versus 1.04 for Minimal-tier vendors. AI maturity is the prerequisite for outcome pricing.

**4. Misaligned organizational incentives.** In many MDR providers, pricing decisions are owned by finance or product management teams that optimize for margin protection, not by security operations teams that understand what outcomes are achievable. This creates a structural disconnect where the people closest to delivery have the least influence on commercial model design.

**5. Fear of accountability.** Outcome-linked pricing makes vendor performance transparent and has real consequences. If a provider commits to a 30-minute MTTR on critical incidents and fails, the financial penalty is immediate and visible. Many vendors prefer the opacity of flat-rate subscriptions where poor performance is masked and contract renewals are a commercial negotiation rather than a performance evaluation.

**6. Legacy contract structures.** Existing multi-year subscription contracts create inertia. Migrating a large installed base from flat-rate subscriptions to outcome-linked models requires renegotiating hundreds of contracts, retraining sales teams, and rebuilding billing systems, a transformation most vendors underestimate.

### AI Is Changing the Cost Equation, but Buyers Aren't Seeing It

Agentic AI is sharply reducing the per-incident cost of MDR service delivery. Automated triage, AI-accelerated threat hunting, machine-driven response playbooks, and predictive detection are enabling vendors to handle more volume with fewer human analysts. This should, in theory, create a natural transition toward efficiency-sharing or outcome-aligned pricing: the vendor delivers more value at lower cost, and the buyer shares in that efficiency gain.

Instead, most vendors are capturing the AI efficiency dividend entirely as margin improvement. The market average for usage-based alignment (PRC-USG) is just 2.14 out of 5.0, meaning most vendors have no mechanism for buyers to see, let alone benefit from, AI-driven cost reductions. The buyer pays the same subscription fee whether their incidents are handled by a senior analyst spending hours on manual investigation or by an AI agent that resolves the same alert in minutes.

This will not hold. As buyers become more informed about AI capabilities and costs, they will demand transparency into how AI efficiency affects what they pay. The 19 vendors in the Significant AI influence tier (averaging 2.86 across all pricing dimensions versus 1.84 for the Minimal tier) are beginning to build this transparency through AI workload dashboards, cost-per-incident reporting, and graduated pricing tiers that reflect AI utilization. These vendors are not more generous; they are more strategic. By sharing the efficiency dividend early, they build a competitive advantage rooted in trust, transparency, and demonstrable value that subscription-only providers cannot replicate.

### The Composable Model Imperative

The data reveals a clear structural prerequisite for pricing model evolution: composability. Vendors with composable pricing models, where buyers can assemble service packages from modular components, score 2.80 across all pricing dimensions versus 2.16 for subscription-only providers. On the critical PRC-SUC dimension, the gap is even starker: 2.41 versus 1.34.

This is not coincidental. Outcome-based pricing components cannot be bolted onto a flat-rate subscription. They require the architectural flexibility to price different service layers differently: a base detection fee, a response orchestration component, an outcome guarantee overlay, a shared-savings mechanism. Each layer has distinct cost drivers, risk profiles, and measurement requirements.

The 27 composable vendors (28% of the market) are positioned to add outcome-linked components over time. The 64 subscription-only vendors (67%) face a structural rebuild before outcomes can even be priced. As AI continues to change how MDR services are delivered, this divide will widen into a lasting competitive gap.

## Background and Context

The managed detection and response (MDR) market has seen considerable consolidation and commoditization pressure over the past three years. As core detection and response capabilities have become table stakes, pricing has emerged as a primary competitive battleground and a key indicator of vendor positioning.

This assessment evaluated 95 MDR service providers across six pricing dimensions: subscription transparency (PRC-SUB), usage-based alignment (PRC-USG), fixed delivery pricing (PRC-FIX), success and outcome fees (PRC-SUC), composability and model maturity (PRC-COM), and pricing-to-outcomes alignment (PRC-OUT). Each dimension was scored on a 1-5 scale based on evidence from vendor documentation, publicly available pricing information, and contracted engagement terms.

The overall market average pricing maturity score of 2.35 out of 5.0 places the MDR market in the "developing" stage. The strongest dimension, subscription transparency at 3.52, reflects an industry that has reached basic commercial maturity in publishing clear tier structures and feature sets. Every other dimension falls below 3.0, with the two outcome-related dimensions (PRC-SUC at 1.63 and PRC-OUT at 1.77) trailing well behind.

The AI enrichment layer, which assesses how AI operational maturity influences pricing model sophistication, reveals that 0 of 95 vendors reach the Transformative tier, 19 are Significant, 49 are Emerging, and 27 are Minimal. The market average AI pricing influence score of 2.48 out of 5.0 confirms that the industry has not yet translated AI investment into commercial model innovation at scale.

## The Impact

The implications of this pricing maturity deficit are direct and practical for both MDR providers and the organizations they serve.

**For MDR vendors,** the inability to articulate and deliver outcome-based pricing will become a serious competitive problem within the next two to three years. As AI-first vendors use efficiency gains to fund outcome guarantees (breach warranties, MTTR commitments, risk-score-linked pricing), providers that cannot match these commercial structures will compete solely on price and feature lists. This is a losing position in a commoditizing market. The 1.02-point pricing sophistication gap between AI-mature and AI-minimal vendors is already visible in win rates and retention, and it will grow.

**For buyers,** the slow adoption of outcome-based pricing means they continue to bear the full risk of poor vendor performance. A subscription-only MDR contract provides no financial consequence if the vendor fails to detect a breach, responds slowly, or delivers inconsistent coverage. Buyers should actively seek vendors willing to put commercial terms against measurable outcomes, and treat a vendor's refusal to do so as a signal about their confidence in their own capabilities.

**For the broader cybersecurity services market,** the MDR pricing evolution is a leading indicator of a market-wide shift. As AI transforms cost structures across managed security services, the pressure for outcome-aligned pricing will extend to SOC-as-a-Service, vulnerability management, and security consulting. Vendors that develop outcome-based commercial capabilities in MDR will have a transferable advantage across their portfolio.

The platform MDR providers (28 vendors, averaging 2.43 and the highest AI influence at 2.75) are best positioned to lead this transition. Their technology platform investments provide the telemetry infrastructure needed to measure outcomes, and their AI adoption is creating the efficiency surplus needed to fund outcome guarantees. IR-enhanced MDR providers (7 vendors, 2.64 average) have the highest pricing maturity but may be constrained by their smaller scale and incident-dependent revenue streams.

Pureplay MDR providers (15 vendors, averaging 2.14 with the lowest AI influence at 2.13) face the most significant challenge. Without the AI capability to underwrite outcome commitments or the platform telemetry to measure them, pureplay vendors risk being squeezed between platform providers offering outcome guarantees at competitive prices and niche specialists offering deep expertise at premium rates.

## Conclusion

The MDR pricing picture shows a market in transition that has not yet arrived. Subscription transparency is reasonably mature, but outcome-based pricing, the dimension that matters most to buyers, is essentially absent. With 86% of vendors scoring 2.0 or below on success and outcome fees, and a median score of 1.0, the market is not incrementally behind; it has barely started.

AI adoption is the single most predictive variable in pricing model sophistication. The 1.02-point gap between AI-mature and AI-minimal vendors, and the even wider divergence on outcome-specific dimensions (2.58 vs. 1.04 on PRC-OUT), make clear that AI capability is not just correlated with pricing innovation; it enables it. Vendors cannot credibly commit to outcome-based pricing without the AI-driven detection, response, and measurement capabilities to consistently deliver against those commitments.

The path forward requires three parallel investments: AI capability maturity (to underwrite outcome guarantees), pricing architecture modernization (from subscription-only to composable), and measurement infrastructure (to enable transparent, trust-based outcome metrics). Vendors that treat pricing model evolution as a finance exercise rather than a capability development effort will find themselves unable to compete as the market matures.

Product leaders should not wait for buyer pressure to force this transition. The vendors that move first, establishing outcome-linked commercial terms while competitors stick with subscription-only models, will set the standard for what "good" looks like and make it harder for followers to catch up. The data is clear: AI maturity enables pricing innovation, pricing innovation enables competitive differentiation, and competitive differentiation determines long-term market position.

## Acronym Key and Glossary Terms

- **MDR**: Managed Detection and Response
- **MTTD**: Mean Time to Detect. The average time from when a threat enters an environment to when it is identified.
- **MTTR**: Mean Time to Respond. The average time from threat detection to containment and initial remediation.
- **PRC-SUB**: Subscription Transparency. Pricing dimension measuring published pricing, tier clarity, and cost predictability.
- **PRC-USG**: Usage-Based Alignment. Pricing dimension measuring alignment of commercial terms to actual workload and consumption patterns.
- **PRC-FIX**: Fixed Delivery Pricing. Pricing dimension measuring availability of fixed-price retainers and project-based engagement models.
- **PRC-SUC**: Success and Outcome Fees. Pricing dimension measuring adoption of outcome-linked fee structures, breach warranties, and performance-based adjustments.
- **PRC-COM**: Composability and Model Maturity. Pricing dimension measuring overall flexibility, modularity, and buyer choice within the pricing architecture.
- **PRC-OUT**: Pricing-to-Outcomes Alignment. Pricing dimension measuring how directly pricing connects to measurable security outcomes.
- **SLA**: Service Level Agreement
- **SOC**: Security Operations Center
- **AI/ML**: Artificial Intelligence / Machine Learning
- **IR**: Incident Response
