|  ![{"uuid":"9918bfd0-fd37-11f0-ba10-5fc54ef42b4b","version":"6.8.0","id":9}][image4] Key Findings (Required)    Agentic AI can streamline forensic workflows without sacrificing trust. Product leaders who address “The Real” digital chain of custody requirements through systemic process and verifiable outcomes, will put product leaders in a position to develop industry leading Digital Forensics and Incident Response capabilities that operate at a significantly higher pace to keep up with the threat actors.  This approach will account for tradition and lagging  laws that are creating a greater divide between offense and defense. Agentic AI is evolving from a simple "detection" tool into a "methodology" engine. These systems can now explain the exact forensic recipes they follow such as memory dump triggers and artifact searches providing an auditable path from raw telemetry to final conclusion. Specialized models like ForensicLLM outperform general-purpose AI by maintaining a high degree of "procedural rigor." They achieve an 86.6% accuracy rate in source attribution, meaning they can point to the specific file, path, or log entry that supports every claim they make. Although still a research model it is showing the direction in which DFIR needs to go.   The emergence of Digital Forensic Knowledge Graphs (DFKGs) allow AI to visualize complex linkages and timelines. This shifts the focus from "what" happened to "how" the evidence was found, enabling human analysts to verify the logic behind an agent's reasoning in real time. Top-tier incident response providers are now using these agents to provide "incident-response-level" results at machine speed. By documenting every automated step and model interaction, they ensure that the resulting evidence meets the strict Daubert and Federal Rule 901 standards for court admissibility. Startups that are AI first are taking greater risks, to gain an edge over their larger competitors by delving into AI driven and enhanced forensic investigation at a greater rate than the more traditional providers. Their focus has been on the critical path which really looks at the mean time to recover or restoration of service.   ![{"uuid":"9e3f1bd0-fd37-11f0-ba10-5fc54ef42b4b","version":"6.8.0","id":7}][image5] Recommendations (Required)    Incident response leaders should prioritize tools that offer "explainable" workflows. Look for systems that use frameworks like SHAP or LIME to provide a human-readable rationale for every investigative decision. Implement a "tandem operation" model where AI agents handle the massive scale of data ingestion and initial correlation, while human experts focus on validating the acquisition methodology and the final strategic interpretation. Forensic teams must require that all AI-generated reports include a detailed evidence lineage. This should consist of deterministic unique identifiers (UIDs) and a record of the specific system locations (memory, disk, or network) where each artifact was discovered. Ensure your AI infrastructure supports "machine-inclusive" chain of custody. Every action taken by an agent including model versions, prompts, and tool invocations must be timestamped and logged in an immutable audit trail to withstand legal scrutiny.  |

| ----- |
| Digital forensics and incident response (DFIR) is an area that has been somewhat lagging behind other areas of Cybersecurity services in the adoption of AI in a meaningful manner that provides real value to the end client. There is a critical path in each incident that requires speed and precision requiring a highly specialized skill set. There are many practitioners that feel the risk associated with the AI “black-box” far outways the benefits of AI adoption. As a result the more traditional vendors, who have been in this space for longer, and the larger service providers, have tended to focus on the use of agentic AI for the more process driven and report writing elements of their services. Although this does provide value, and does save some time it is not necessarily what should be considered within the critical path of an investigation. The primary focus should always be minimizing risk and time to recovery.  Agentic AI has come a long way in a very short amount of time and will continue to do so.  For DFIR vendors to remain relevant, product leaders will need to focus the development of their capabilities on taking advantage of AI where it will be able to have the greatest impact on the investigation, forensic analysis process. Agentic AI is at the point where it can handle the far more complex tasks at scale, precision and with extreme consistency 80% to 90% of the time, that is simply not possible when done manually.   |
